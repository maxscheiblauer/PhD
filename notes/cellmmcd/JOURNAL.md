# cellMMCD journal (append-only, dated)

## 2026-11-04, block 1: candidate formulations and the choice

**Why RJ cannot be the cellwise method.** RJ trims whitened columns z_ij = X_i a_j. A single bad cell (k,l) of X_i enters coordinate k of *every* z_ij (j = 1..q), so RJ must trim all q columns of X_i, i.e. the whole matrix. With ε bad cells, P(matrix clean) = (1-ε)^{pq}: 28 % for ε = .1, pq = 12. That is the 10 % breakdown in `output/FINDINGS.md` §4. Any method whose trimming unit contains many cells has the same problem. The unit must be the cell.

**Candidates considered.**
1. *Product-set deletion* (drop whole rows and columns of X_i so the remaining block stays matrix-normal, N(0, Σᶜ_CC ⊗ Σʳ_RR)): closed-form Σʳ step, but each bad cell costs a full row or column; at ε = .1 half the data goes. Rejected.
2. *Trimmed pseudo-likelihood* (product of full conditionals, each cell as response, trimmed per cell): cheap, but a bad covariate cell corrupts the conditional of every good cell in its row and column (propagation), and the pseudo-likelihood is not the likelihood. Kept as fallback.
3. *Pairwise Kronecker estimator* (robust scale per cell position, robust GK correlation per pair of positions in the same row or the same column, then the Kronecker structure gives Σʳ, Σᶜ directly): O(n(p²q+pq²)), no imputation, no iteration, consistent at the Gaussian with the usual GK factors. Low efficiency, not a likelihood. **Chosen as the initial estimator** (`kpair()`).
4. *Trimmed observed-cell likelihood with Kronecker parameter* = the cellMCD objective (Raymaekers & Rousseeuw 2024) with Σ = Σᶜ ⊗ Σʳ. **Chosen as the method, "cellMMCD".**

**The objective.** Cells flagged in X_i form M_i, the rest O_i; x_i = vec(X_i - M). With λ_kl ≥ 0 and a quota of at least h = ⌈αn⌉ unflagged cells per position (k,l):

F(Σʳ, Σᶜ, W) = Σ_i [ log det Σ_{O_iO_i} + x_{iO_i}' Σ_{O_iO_i}^{-1} x_{iO_i} + |O_i| log 2π ] + Σ_{i,k,l} λ_kl (1 - w_ikl).

Special cases: W ≡ 1 gives the matrix-normal likelihood (flip-flop MLE); flagging whole matrices gives a trimmed-observation likelihood (MMCD-type). No cell value is ever replaced: the estimator is the marginal likelihood of the cells that are kept.

**The identity that makes it cheap (proven, elementary).** For Σ = Σᶜ⊗Σʳ, Ω = Σ^{-1} = Ωᶜ⊗Ωʳ, Y_i = X_i - M with flagged cells set to 0, T_i = Ωʳ Y_i Ωᶜ... more precisely T_i = Ωʳ (X_i - M) Ωᶜ computed on the full matrix, and u_i = (Ωʳ Y_i Ωᶜ)[M_i]:
- det Σ_{OO} = det Σ · det Ω_{MM}, so log det Σ_{O_iO_i} = q log|Σʳ| + p log|Σᶜ| + log det Ω_{M_iM_i};
- x_O' Σ_OO^{-1} x_O = x_O' (Ω_OO - Ω_OM Ω_MM^{-1} Ω_MO) x_O = tr(Ωʳ Y_i Ωᶜ Y_i') - u_i' Ω_{M_iM_i}^{-1} u_i;
- Ω_{MM} = [Ωʳ_{k_a k_b} Ωᶜ_{l_a l_b}]_{ab} is an m_i × m_i matrix read off the factors.
Cost per observation O(p²q + pq² + m_i³) instead of O((pq)³). In the cellwise regime m_i ≈ εpq is small.

**Cell step (exact block coordinate descent).** For a cell c of observation i, with S = M_i ∪ {c}, the conditional law of x_c given the other observed cells has variance σ²_c = (Ω_SS^{-1})_cc and residual r_c = (Ω_SS^{-1} T_S)_c / σ_c (T = Ω x on the full vector; this holds whether c is currently flagged or not). The change of F from unflagging c is Δ_c - λ_kl with Δ_c = log(2πσ²_c) + r_c². F is separable over i given the parameters, so for one position (k,l) the exact minimizer over w_{·kl} flags the cells with the largest Δ - λ > 0, at most n - h of them. Sweeping the positions is therefore monotone. With m_i = 0 the rule reads: flag iff the full-conditional standardized residual (Ωʳ(X_i-M)Ωᶜ)_kl / sqrt(Ωʳ_kk Ωᶜ_ll) is large; that is the Kronecker version of the DDC/cellMCD residual. λ_kl = χ²_{1,quant} + log 2π + log(1/(Ωʳ_kk Ωᶜ_ll)) at the initial estimate, as in cellMCD.

**Parameter step and the EM question (owner guideline 2).** Given W, the first-order condition for Σʳ is Σʳ = (nq)^{-1} Σ_i E[X_i Ωᶜ X_i' | x_{iO_i}], and likewise for Σᶜ, because the observed-data score equals the conditional expectation of the complete-data score (Fisher's identity). So *every* first-order method for this objective evaluates the same conditional-mean and conditional-covariance terms; "EM" here is nothing but the plain fixed-point iteration of the exact score, and with the identity above its cost is O(p²q + pq² + m_i³) per observation, not the O((pq)³) that made EM look prohibitive. I therefore use one flip-flop sweep of this fixed point per outer iteration (a GEM step, so F does not increase). This does not violate guideline 1: the estimator is defined by the observed-cell likelihood and never replaces a cell by a predicted value; the conditional means appear only inside the exact score. The non-EM alternative (gradient step with line search on the same F) uses the same quantities and only adds a step-size search; I see no cheaper exact alternative and record that as the reason.

**Convergence (proven, given boundedness).** F never increases, W takes finitely many values, and for fixed W the parameter step is monotone, so the sequence F_t converges; W stabilizes after finitely many steps. Open: a condition on h, p, q under which F is bounded below (RJ needed h ≥ q+1; here I conjecture h ≥ max(p,q)+1 per position suffices, to be checked numerically).

**Plan for this block.** Implement `kpair`, the objective with a brute-force check, the cell step with a brute-force check of Δ, the parameter step, `cellmmcd()`; tests; one experiment on the FINDINGS cellwise setting (p = 3, q = 4, n = 100, 6-sd cells) against FF, RJ, MMCD, kpair and cellMCD on vec(X) + nearest Kronecker.

## 2026-11-04, block 2: the scale fixed point, λ-calibration, casewise mix

**Plan.** (1) Explain the clean-data scale ratio 0.895 and remove it without breaking the objective. (2) Casewise + cellwise contamination. (3) Existence grid. (4) Method note update.

**Why the scale shrinks (1-D proxy, proven in the proxy).** Take one cell position, true conditional variance 1, working (current) scale t, initial scale t₀. The cell step flags iff Δ − λ > 0, Δ = log(2πt) + x²/t, λ = c + log 2π + log t₀, c = χ²_{1,quant}; i.e. iff x² > a(t) := t(c + log(t₀/t)). The parameter step replaces a flagged cell by its conditional mean (0 in the proxy) plus its conditional variance t, so t' = E[x²1{x² ≤ a}] + t P(x² > a) = F₃(a) + t(1 − F₁(a)) with F_k the χ²_k cdf. The fixed point satisfies t* = F₃(a*)/F₁(a*), a* = t*(c + log(t₀/t*)). For quant = .99, t₀ = 1 this gives t* ≈ 0.90 with 1 − F₁(a*) ≈ 1.4 % flagged (the naive F₃(c)/F₁(c) = 0.925 ignores that the threshold a = ct shrinks with t). Observed: 0.895 ± 0.004 and 1.7 % (checked numerically, block 1). The Kronecker conditional residuals are exactly N(0,1) at the truth when m_i = 0, so the proxy is the leading-order description of the global scale; the shapes are unaffected in expectation (positions are flagged symmetrically), consistent with the shape KL going to 0 with n.

**Remedy: λ-calibration (keeps the objective).** Choose the penalty so that at the proxy fixed point exactly the nominal fraction is flagged: with t₁ = F₃(c)/F₁(c),
λ_kl = c/t₁ + log t₁ + log 2π − log(Ωʳ_kk Ωᶜ_ll) at the initial estimate, and report Σ/t₁ at the end.
Check in the proxy: at t = t₁ the rule reads x²/t₁ > c/t₁ + log t₁ + log(t₀/t₁) = c/t₁ (t₀ = 1), i.e. x² > c, flagged fraction 1 − quant; then t' = F₃(c) + t₁(1 − F₁(c)) = t₁. So t₁ is the fixed point and Σ/t₁ is Fisher-consistent for the scale (proven in the proxy; to be checked numerically in the Kronecker model). This is a legitimate member of the same family of objectives (a different λ), so the monotone descent and convergence arguments are untouched; the factor 1/t₁ is applied once at the end, exactly as the MCD consistency factor. Rejected alternative: scaling the working covariance inside the cell step, which makes the cell step minimize a different function than the parameter step (no monotone objective).

**Results of the calibration (`exp/cm_step3_calib.R`, `output/cm_step3_calib.log`).** Proxy fixed point: uncalibrated t* = 0.905 with 1.35 % flagged (observed in block 1: 0.895, 1.7 %; the Kronecker model flags ~0.3 % more than the proxy, partly the forced marginal flags). **Efficiency at the Gaussian (`exp/cm_step3b_lambda.R`, `exp/cm_step3c_eff.R`).** Remaining gap to the MLE at 3×4, n = 1000: KL ratio 2.4. Not the start and not the noise of λ: with the true Σ as start and λ the KL is identical (0.0173 vs 0.0173). Not the forced marginal flags (removing them: ratio 2.7). Open.

**Observation step (`cm_vstep`, block 2).** Third block of the same objective: observation weights v_i ∈ {0,1}, F = Σ_i v_i d_i + μ Σ_i (1 − v_i), at most n − h_obs trimmed, h_obs = ⌈α_obs n⌉. Exact minimisation over v given (Σ, W): trim the observations with the largest d_i − μ > 0. μ = q log|Σʳ₀| + p log|Σᶜ₀| + pq log 2π + χ²_{pq,.99} at the initial estimate (an observation is trimmed when its full-data log-likelihood contribution is beyond the .99 quantile; cells already flagged enter d_i through their penalty λ, i.e. as if sitting at their own .99 conditional quantile), calibrated like λ with t_obs = F_{pq+2}(c)/F_{pq}(c), final Σ divided by t₁ t_obs. μ = −∞ gives the hard quota (classical trimmed likelihood, MMCD-type). Order per iteration: v-step, cell step over the kept observations (quota h = ⌈α n_v⌉), parameter step on the kept observations. Monotone (tested, 13/13), exact v-step (brute-force test).

**Why cells alone fail at 20 % casewise shift (`exp/cm_step4_mixed.R`, p = q = 5, n = 100).** A constant shift of all cells is largely explained by the conditional means (the AR correlations put much of the shift into the regression on the neighbours), so only 68 % of the cells of a shifted matrix get flagged and the rest bias Σ: KL 2.48 (cells only) vs MMCD 0.21. With the penalised observation step: all shifted/scaled matrices and 85–95 % of the structural ones (drawn with flipped correlation signs) are trimmed; KL 0.36 (shift .20), 0.28 (scale .20), 0.20–0.25 at 10 % casewise, against MMCD 0.18–0.27 and cells-only 0.29–0.95. In the mixed setting (10 % shifted matrices + 5 % bad cells) cellMMCD is the only working method (0.43 vs MMCD 51). A bug in the hard-quota ordering (μ = −∞ made every score +∞) was found through this experiment and fixed; rerun pending at the time of writing.

**Existence: the objective is unbounded under the position quota alone (proven, data in general position).** Let p, q ≥ 2 and suppose n − h ≥ n / max(p,q). Then inf F = −∞. Proof (p = max(p,q); the other case is symmetric). Pick one column (i₀,l₀) and a unit vector a ⊥ x_{i₀,·,l₀} (generic, full support). Let Σʳ(δ) = I − (1−δ)aa', Σᶜ = I. Choose W: keep column (i₀,l₀) fully; give every other column (i,l) at least one flag. This needs at most n flags among the p positions of column l, i.e. p(n − h) ≥ n, which is the hypothesis. For i ≠ i₀ every column of X_i has a flag, and since a has full support no vector w ⊗ a is supported on O_i, so (Σᶜ⊗Σʳ(0))_{O_iO_i} is nonsingular and d_i(δ) stays bounded. For i₀, e_{l₀}⊗a is a null vector of the limit supported on O_{i₀}, so log det Σ_{OO}(δ) → −∞ like log δ, while x_O ⊥ e_{l₀}⊗a keeps the quadratic form bounded (the perturbation δ(I⊗aa') is positive on that kernel, so the 1/δ term of the inverse is annihilated by x_O). The penalties are finite. Hence F → −∞. ∎ Consequences: (i) at α = .75 the objective has no minimiser as soon as max(p,q) ≥ 4, and at α = .5 never; (ii) the same counting applies to the unstructured cellMCD objective with d = pq (null vector of full support, every row needs a flag, d(n − h) ≥ n − (d − 1)); to be checked against the constraints in Raymaekers & Rousseeuw (2024), which I could not consult here; (iii) what the algorithm computes is a local minimiser reached from the kpair start. Grid (`exp/cm_step5_exist.R`, 4 seeds, clean data): the descent does reach degenerate solutions when n is small (5×5: half of the runs at n = 12, all at n ≤ 9, α = .75; 8×8: 3 of 4 at n = 12), never at n = 100 in any experiment so far. Converse (conjectured): if n − h < n / max(p,q), F is bounded below for data in general position; the grid is only partly consistent with it (3×3, h = 7 > 2n/3 at n = 9: one of four runs ended degenerate by the indicator). Remedy (conjectured sufficient): require at least p fully kept columns (i,l) for every l and q fully kept rows for every k; p fully kept columns in general position leave no null vector a. Not implemented: it couples the positions in the cell step. First action for block 3.

## 2026-11-04, block 3: the efficiency gap is (partly) a bias; censoring vs trimming; reweighting

**Plan.** (1) Rerun the cellwise benchmark with the calibrated default and the observation step. (2) Explain the efficiency gap at the Gaussian (KL ratio 2.4 at quant .99, n = 1000). (3) Practical remedy. (4) Degenerate descents if budget allows.

**Rerun of `exp/cm_step1.R` (`output/cm_step1.log`; block 1's log kept as `cm_step1_block1.log`).** Clean data: KL 0.125 / 0.254 (3×4 / 5×5; was 0.166 / 0.37), tr ratio 0.999 / 0.991 (was 0.896), false flags 1.5–1.7 %. Contaminated: unchanged within noise (recall 0.97–1.00, false flags 0.4–1.3 %, KL 0.10–0.26 at 3×4 and 5×5 for ε ≤ .10). The observation step did not trim clean matrices in these runs. METHOD §9 updated.

**Why the gap: the trimming is not at random (first-order calculation, then checked numerically).** A clean cell is flagged when its standardized conditional residual $r_c = (x_c - m_c(x_{-c}))/\sigma_c$ exceeds $c$ in absolute value, and the parameter step then treats it as *missing*: conditional mean plus conditional variance $\sigma_c^2$. But a flagged clean cell is a tail cell, $E[r_c^2 \mid |r_c| > c] = (1 - F_3(c))/(1 - F_1(c)) = 8.45 \gg 1$. Population fixed-point map at the truth, one cell $c$, kept set $O = \{-c\}$: $x_c = m_c + \sigma_c r_c$ with $r_c \perp x_{-c}$; the second-moment update of $\Sigma_{cc}$ is $E[x_c^2 1\{|r_c|\le c\}] + E[(m_c^2 + \sigma_c^2)1\{|r_c|>c\}] = \Sigma_{cc} - \sigma_c^2 (F_1(c) - F_3(c))$, while every cross moment $E[\hat x_c x_j] = \Sigma_{cj}$ is unchanged (the tail is symmetric). Hence, to first order in $\kappa = F_1(c) - F_3(c) = 0.0745$ (quant .99), the fixed point is
$$\Sigma^\* = \Sigma - \kappa\,\operatorname{diag}(1/\Omega_{cc}),$$
which is a uniform scale only when all conditional variances are equal (e.g. $\Sigma = I$). In the Kronecker model $\operatorname{diag}(1/\Omega_{cc}) = D^r \otimes D^c$ with $D^r = \operatorname{diag}(1/\Omega^r_{kk})$, and the flip-flop step projects $\Sigma^r\otimes\Sigma^c - \kappa D^r\otimes D^c$ onto the Kronecker set. The $t_1$ calibration of block 2 removes the trace part; the remaining shape bias costs, after removing the best uniform scale, roughly $\tfrac14\kappa^2 \sum_{i\ne j}\mathrm{pcor}_{ij}^2$ in KL, which does not vanish with $n$ (a bias, not a variance). At quant .999, $\kappa = 0.0117$, so the bias term is 40 times smaller. Status: **proven to first order (population map at the truth, one flagged cell per matrix)**; numerically checked by the $n$-scaling experiment below.

**Two remedies.**
1. *Censoring instead of deletion (`tail = TRUE`, `cm_pstep(tau)`).* Treat a flagged cell as *censored* (known to lie beyond its cutoff) rather than missing: in the parameter step multiply the conditional covariance $C_i = \Omega_{M_iM_i}^{-1}$ of the flagged cells by $\tau = (1 - F_3(c))/(1 - F_1(c))$ (the conditional mean is unchanged by symmetry). In the 1-D proxy the fixed point is then at scale exactly 1 with exactly $1 - $quant flagged, without the $t_1$ factor (`cm_proxy_fixpt(tau = )`, proven in the proxy, test 14), and the first-order multivariate bias vanishes by the same computation. The value of an outlying cell never enters (only the bounded constant $\tau\sigma_c^2$), so the influence of a flagged cell stays bounded. This is the EM step of the *censored-cell likelihood* $\prod_i f(x_{iO_i}) \prod_{c\in M_i} P_\Sigma(|r_{ic}| > c \mid x_{iO_i})$, which in the 1-D proxy has its stationary point at the true scale (identity $F_1(c) - F_3(c) = 2cf_1(c)$). **Cost:** the cell step is still coordinate descent on the trimmed objective $F$, and the parameter step no longer decreases $F$ (test 16 only checks convergence). A monotone formulation would define the cutoff region in $x$-space at the initial estimate and use the censored objective in both steps (open; the joint tail region of several flagged cells has no closed-form moments, $\tau C$ is an approximation for $m_i \ge 2$).
2. *Reweighting (`reweight = TRUE`, `cm_reweight`).* The reweighted-MCD device: at the raw fit, re-decide every cell with the higher cutoff $\chi^2_{1,.999}$ on its residual given the other kept cells of the raw fit (forced marginal flags kept, no quota), keep the observation weights $v$, then run parameter steps with $(v,W)$ fixed (EM, monotone, the observed-cell MLE given the flags), and divide the scale by $t_{rw} = F_3(c_{rw})/F_1(c_{rw})$ and $t_{obs}$. The flags are decided at the robust raw fit, so a 6-sd cell is still caught (test 15: recall 1.00, clean flags 0.6 %). The remaining first-order bias is $\kappa(.999)^2$-small.

**Full-column safeguard (`cm_fullcol`, `exp/cm_step7_safeguard.R`): negative result.** Restoring p fully kept columns per l and q fully kept rows per k after each cell step (unflagging the kept observations with the fewest flags) does **not** prevent the degenerate descents at n ≤ 12 (degenerate fraction unchanged or higher, e.g. 5×5 n = 12: 0.00 → 0.75; 8×8 n = 20: 0.50 → 1.00), unflags thousands of cells at 8×8 (it fights the cell step at every iteration), and breaks monotonicity in 25–100 % of the runs. KL improves at n ≤ 12 only because fewer cells are flagged. So the conjectured remedy, implemented as a post-hoc repair, is not sufficient; either the conjecture is wrong (p fully kept columns per l do not bound F; the degeneracy may come from Σᶜ and Σʳ jointly) or the degeneracy indicator (eigenvalue ratio < 1e-6 or F < F₀ − 50n) flags legitimate near-singular fits at n = 6–12 (the MLE itself has KL 3–9 there). Kept as an option (`safeguard = FALSE` by default); abandoned as a direction. ## 2026-11-04, block 4: lock-in between the two trimming steps; shadow cell step

**Observation (cm_step8, block 3).** With 10 % of the cells at 3–4 sd in *every* matrix, the observation step trims its full quota of 25 % of the matrices, in every variant. Mechanism (read off the driver): the observation step runs first in each iteration; at iteration 1 the flags are only the marginal ones (W = Wfix, |x|/MAD > 3, which catches about half of the 3-sd cells), so the 2–3 unflagged shifted cells of a matrix add ≈ 8–15 to its kept-cell Mahalanobis distance, more than 25 % of the matrices exceed μ_i (χ²_{25,.99} = 44.3 against a mean of ≈ 35), and the quota binds. Afterwards the lock-in: `cm_wstep` only decides cells of *kept* observations, so a trimmed matrix keeps W_i = Wfix_i, its d_i − μ_i stays positive at every later observation step, and it is never re-admitted, although its cells would have been flagged at once had it been kept.

**Idea: shadow cell step.** The λ terms cancel in d_i − μ_i (both charge Σ_{M_i} λ), so the observation step compares the kept-cell likelihood with its own threshold; what the trimmed matrix needs is a current W_i. Let the cell step also re-decide the cells of the trimmed observations by the same rule Δ_ic − λ_kl > 0 (coordinate descent on their own d_i, no quota: they do not count toward anything while trimmed). Then the next observation step sees the matrix with its cellwise outliers flagged and re-admits it if the rest is fine; the majority rule still trims a shifted matrix (most of its cells get shadow-flagged). Monotonicity: a trimmed observation enters F only through μ_i(W_i), so F can move either way while shadow flags change; hence the shadow step is run only in the first `shadow_it` = 10 iterations, after which the algorithm is the block-3 one and the convergence argument (finite (v, W), monotone F) applies verbatim after at most 10 non-monotone iterations. Why not simply run the cell step before the observation step at iteration 1: the lock-in would recur for every matrix trimmed later, and the per-position quota would then be computed over casewise outliers too (the block-2 failure). Why not alpha_obs = 1: loses the casewise protection (checked in cm_step9 alongside, as the brief's "ideally casewise too" is a secondary goal).

**Result (cm_step9, p = q = 5, n = 100, median KL / trimmed fraction).** Clean: block 3 0.198 / 0.04 (it trims 4 % of clean matrices!), shadow 0.182 / 0.00. 10 % cells at 3 sd: 0.335 / 0.25 vs 0.332 / 0.00; at 4 sd: 0.297 / 0.25 vs 0.259 / 0.00; at 6 sd 0.249 / 0.02 vs 0.254 / 0.00. 20 % shifted matrices (5 sd): 0.372 / 0.23 vs 0.344 / 0.20 (exactly the shifted ones, recall 1.00, false flags 0.6 %); mixed 10 % matrices + 5 % cells: 0.205 / 0.14 vs 0.222 / 0.10. The alternatives: alpha_obs = 1 loses the casewise protection (KL 1.96 at 20 % shifted matrices, recall 0.67), quant_obs = .999 under-trims shifted matrices (0.17, KL 0.51). **Decision: shadow_it = 10 is the default.** F was monotone after the shadow phase in every run checked (test 18); during it, it is not guaranteed.

## 2026-11-04, block 4: influence functions in the 1-D proxy (proven in the proxy; checked numerically to 1e-4)

Proxy as in block 2: one cell, residual distribution F, N_F(a) = ∫x²1(x²≤a)dF, D_F(a) = ∫1(x²≤a)dF; at Φ, N = F₃, D = F₁ (χ² cdfs). Start t₀(F) = squared standardized MAD, IF_{t₀}(x) = 2·sign(|x| − q₃)/(4q₃φ(q₃)) = ±2.333 (q₃ = Φ⁻¹(3/4)); the location (median) enters the even scale functionals only at second order and is ignored.

**Raw estimator.** T(F) solves ψ(t;F) = N_F(a) + t(1 − D_F(a)) − t = 0 with a = t(c/t₁ + log t₁ + log(t₀/t)); calibrated raw scale T_raw = T/t₁. At Φ: t = t₁, a = c, t₀ = 1. Gateaux derivative of ψ in the direction δ_x − Φ: 1(x²≤c)(x² − t₁) (using t₁F₁(c) = F₃(c)). ∂ψ/∂a = φ(√a)(√a − t/√a), ∂a/∂t = c/t₁ − 1, ∂a/∂t₀ = t₁. Hence
IF_raw(x) = −[1(x²≤c)(x² − t₁) + t₁φ(√c)(c − t₁)/√c · IF_{t₀}(x)] / {t₁[(c − t₁)²φ(√c)/(t₁√c) − F₁(c)]}.
Quant .99: denominator t₁·(0.198 − 0.990) = −0.733; IF_raw(x) = [1(x²≤c)(x² − 0.925) + 0.0297·IF_{t₀}(x)]/0.733. Bounded; maximum 7.36 just below √c = 2.576; beyond the cutoff only the start transmits: 0.094 for all |x| > √c.

**Reweighted estimator.** T_rw(F) = N_F(a_rw)/D_F(a_rw)/t_rw, a_rw = c_rw·T_raw(F). Quotient rule at Φ, with dN/da = √a φ(√a), dD/da = φ(√a)/√a, t_rw = F₃(c_rw)/F₁(c_rw):
IF_rw(x) = 1(x²≤c_rw)[x²/F₃(c_rw) − 1/F₁(c_rw)] + √c_rw φ(√c_rw)(c_rw F₁ − F₃)/(F₁F₃) · IF_raw(x).
Quant_rw .999: transmitted coefficient 0.058; IF_rw(x) ≈ 1(x² ≤ 10.83)(1.013x² − 1.001) + 0.058·IF_raw(x). Gross-error sensitivity 9.97 (just below √c_rw = 3.29, as for the reweighted MCD at its cutoff); IF_rw(x) = 0.0055 for |x| > √c_rw: a gross cell has (essentially) no influence, the small residue comes through the MAD start. MLE for comparison: IF = x² − 1, unbounded. Check: `cm_proxy_T` evaluates both functionals exactly under (1 − ε)Φ + εδ_x (closed-form integrals, ε = 1e-6); formulas and numerics agree to 1e-4 at x ∈ {0, 0.5, …, 10} (test 19). Caveat: this is the proxy (one cell, known conditioning); in the Kronecker model the cell's conditional variance and the other cells' flags enter too, and the matrix-level observation step adds a second bounded piece; the proxy says what happens along one cell's residual, which is the mechanism that mattered for the bias (block 3).

## 2026-11-04, block 5: a monotone formulation of the shadow phase? (STATE item 2; negative, on paper)

F = Σ_kept d_i(W_i) + Σ_trimmed μ_i(W_i), with d_i(W) = kept-cell nll + Σ_{M_i}λ and μ_i(W) = thr(|O_i|) + Σ_{M_i}λ, where thr(m) = log det Σ₀,OO + m log 2π + χ²_{m,.99}/t + m log t. The shadow step changes W_i of trimmed matrices, hence μ_i(W_i), hence F, in either direction. The obvious repair, a *constant* trimmed charge μ̄_i (e.g. μ_i(Wfix_i), or min_W μ_i(W)), makes F monotone, but then the exact v-step compares d_i(W_i) with μ̄_i, and
d_i(W_i) − μ_i(Wfix_i) = [kept nll − thr(|O_i|)] + Σ_{M_i∖M_i^fix} [λ_kl − (thr increment per kept cell ≈ log 2πσ² + 1.5–2)] ≈ (kept-cell excess) + (#new flags) × (c − 2) ≈ excess + 4.6 × #new flags.
So every shadow flag costs ≈ 4.6 units of deviance against re-admission: a matrix with three 4-sd cells (excess ≈ −10 once flagged) stays trimmed. **A constant trimmed charge is the same as charging flagged cells in the trimming decision**, which is the block-2 dead end (12.5 % of the matrices trimmed under 5 % cellwise contamination). Any charge that is a function of W_i and makes F monotone under the shadow updates must be non-increasing in the flags along the shadow descent, i.e. it is a charge μ̃_i ≤ μ_i(W_i) that flags make *cheaper*, which then re-admits every matrix (observation step disabled). Conclusion: the "flags are free for the trimming decision" principle is incompatible with a monotone joint objective during the shadow phase. The shadow phase therefore remains what it is: a finite (≤ shadow_it = 10 iterations) non-monotone preprocessing of (v, W), after which the block-coordinate descent of §4 on F is monotone and converges; the estimator is the limit of that descent from the preprocessed state (well defined; the dependence on shadow_it is a tuning constant like the number of C-steps in FAST-MCD's raw phase). Marked in METHOD §8 as closed with a negative result.
